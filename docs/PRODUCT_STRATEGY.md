# Product strategy

## Positioning
**Telecontact Intelligence — Procurement Intelligence** is a commercial intelligence workstation for companies that sell to public buyers. It does not stop at listing tenders: it explains what the records mean commercially — who buys, how often, who wins, how concentrated each segment is, which needs will recur, which partners can execute, and whether a given tender deserves bid resources.

This repository is a **prototype on synthetic data**. It demonstrates the product vision, analytical method and UX. It is not an officially launched or approved Telecontact product.

## Target users
| User | Main question | Where it is answered |
|---|---|---|
| Business development lead | Where should we hunt next quarter? | Overview, Market Intelligence, Future Signals |
| Bid manager | Should we bid on this tender, and how? | Opportunity dossier, Bid Workspace, Bid Readiness report |
| Partnerships manager | Who can execute with us, in which country? | Suppliers & Partners, Provider Acquisition |
| Executive | How is the market moving and where do we stand? | Overview, Market report, Awardees |
| Analyst | Can I trust these figures? | Data Quality workspace, evidence links, methodology |

## The questions the product answers (from the brief)
1. Which buyers purchase our services — buyer × sector matrix, buyer profiles.
2. Which sectors are most active — sector breakdown, 12-month momentum.
3. Which regions are attractive — regional demand, region × sector matrix.
4. Who repeatedly wins similar contracts — awardee leaderboards, dossier awardees.
5. Who competes in a sector — competitor directory, head-to-head, participants on similar tenders.
6. Which buyers have recurring needs — purchasing lines, recurrence share, signals.
7. Who can supply or execute — supplier matching in each dossier.
8. Which relationships to develop — supply–demand gap, buyer dependence, acquisition pipeline.
9. What to investigate before responding — dossier risks and missing information.
10. Which needs may emerge — Future Signals.
11. Is an opportunity commercially interesting — readiness score and posture.
12. How to improve bid strategy — incumbent analysis, value positioning, recommended actions.
13. Which partners can help in different countries — network footprint and coverage.

## Product principles
- **Intelligence over listings.** Every page leads with an interpretation computed from records.
- **Traceability.** Every metric shows sample size and period; findings link to record ids.
- **Honesty about uncertainty.** Statements are typed (fact / metric / interpretation / recommendation / assumption / unknown); small samples are flagged; signals are not announcements.
- **Bids ≠ awards.** Participation and award data are never conflated.
- **Network as a first-class asset.** Market demand is joined to the supplier network to reveal execution gaps.

## Value hypothesis
Bid teams waste effort on tenders with entrenched incumbents, unreliable buyers or no execution partner. The dossier surfaces those factors before effort is spent, and the signal engine moves business development earlier — before publication.

## Success measures for a pilot (future)
Share of bids preceded by a dossier review; win rate on dossier-recommended bids vs others; lead time between signal and publication; number of active partners per sector with demand.
